## H-2: a strategy to ensure business operational continuity in the United States

Over the past few years, the U.S. labor market has undergone one of the most significant shifts in decades. The availability of temporary labor has become a decisive factor for productivity in strategic sectors such as agriculture, agribusiness, construction, food processing, hospitality, tourism, maintenance, landscaping, and various service industries.

For thousands of companies in states like **California, Texas, and Florida**, the main challenge is no longer solely about increasing production or expanding installed capacity. The real challenge lies in ensuring the availability of skilled workers during peak-demand seasons.

This reality has turned workforce planning into a strategic element for organizations' operational continuity.

## The H-2 program: a legal mechanism for temporary needs

In this context, the U.S. Government's H-2 program represents one of the most important legal mechanisms for meeting temporary staffing needs. Through the **H-2A** category, aimed at the agricultural sector, and the **H-2B** category, geared toward temporary non-agricultural activities, employers can hire foreign workers when they demonstrate that there is not enough U.S. labor available to fill those positions.

According to the U.S. Department of Labor, the process begins with obtaining a **Temporary Labor Certification**, through which the employer proves the need to hire foreign personnel and demonstrates that such hiring will not negatively affect the wages or working conditions of U.S. workers. The petition is then filed with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), the agency responsible for authorizing employment under the H-2 program.

## Beyond immigration: a human capital strategy

Although the H-2 program is often analyzed from an immigration perspective, for employers it primarily represents a **human capital management strategy**. Having workers available at the right time makes it possible to maintain operational continuity, reduce the risks associated with production delays, strengthen compliance with commercial contracts, and plan peak-activity seasons with greater certainty.

In industries where timing determines profitability, the availability of personnel can mean the difference between fulfilling a contract or losing an entire production season.

## The role of the Mexican workforce

The experience of recent years shows that Mexican labor continues to play a fundamental role within U.S. supply chains. Its expertise in agricultural activities, food processing, maintenance, hospitality, landscaping, and other industries has allowed thousands of companies to maintain competitive productivity levels under a hiring framework fully regulated by federal authorities.

## Benefits for both parties

Beyond legal compliance, the H-2 program generates benefits for both sides. Employers gain a pre-selected workforce, with the experience and availability to join during specific production periods. At the same time, workers gain access to formal employment opportunities, wages regulated by U.S. law, and working conditions supervised by the competent authorities.

## Workforce planning as a strategic decision

From a business standpoint, international temporary hiring should no longer be understood as a reactive measure to fill last-minute vacancies. Today it represents a **strategic decision** that requires analysis, planning, and proper operational risk management.

Companies that incorporate workforce planning into their annual strategy are better positioned to successfully face the challenges posed by labor shortages, seasonal fluctuations, and the new dynamics of the labor market.
